    Testosterone, Free, Bioavailable and Total, LC/MS

    $65.00

    This panel provides an in-depth analysis of testosterone levels and availability in the body. It evaluates total production, how much is free (active), and how much is biologically available for use. It’s essential for assessing hormonal health, performance, fertility, libido, mood, and overall vitality. Included markers: Testosterone, Total (MS) – The total amount of testosterone in the bloodstream, measured via mass spectrometry for high accuracy. Includes both bound and unbound testosterone. Uncapped. Testosterone, Free – The portion of testosterone not bound to proteins, representing the biologically active form that can enter cells and exert effects. Testosterone, Bioavailable – The sum of free testosterone plus the portion loosely bound to albumin—both forms readily available for use in the body. Sex Hormone Binding Globulin (SHBG) – A protein that binds tightly to testosterone. High SHBG can reduce free and bioavailable testosterone, even when total levels are normal. Albumin – A protein that loosely binds testosterone. Albumin levels are used to calculate bioavailable testosterone.

    Blood & Iron Markers

    ABO Group and Rh Type

    $18.50

    ABO Grouping and Rho(D) Typing are essential blood tests used to determine an individual's blood type and Rh factor. ABO Grouping identifies whether a person’s blood group is A, B, AB, or O based on the presence or absence of A and B antigens on red blood cells, along with corresponding antibodies in the plasma. Rho(D) Typing determines if the Rh factor (Rho\[D] antigen) is present on the red blood cells, classifying the person as Rh-positive or Rh-negative. These tests are critical for ensuring compatibility in blood transfusions, managing Rh incompatibility during pregnancy to prevent hemolytic disease of the newborn, guiding organ donation, and supporting emergency medical situations when blood type information is needed.

    Lipids & Cardiovascular Risk

    Advanced Lipid Panel, Cardio IQ

    $100.00

    This panel provides a deep analysis of your cholesterol particle size, density, and number—going far beyond standard lipid testing. It’s designed for individuals seeking a more accurate assessment of cardiovascular risk, especially when traditional cholesterol markers appear normal but risk may still be elevated. Key markers include: Total Cholesterol, HDL, LDL, Triglycerides – Core lipid values used to assess general heart health. Cholesterol/HDL Ratio – A risk metric; lower ratios are generally better. Non-HDL Cholesterol – Total cholesterol minus HDL; includes all atherogenic (harmful) lipoproteins. Lipoprotein (a) – A genetically influenced lipoprotein that increases risk for atherosclerosis and heart attack, independent of LDL or HDL levels. Apolipoprotein B (ApoB) – Measures the actual number of atherogenic particles; a more accurate marker of cardiovascular risk than LDL alone. LDL Particle Number (LDL-P) – Indicates how many LDL particles are in your blood. High particle count, even with normal LDL levels, raises risk. LDL Peak Size – Reflects the size of LDL particles. Smaller, denser particles are more dangerous than larger ones. LDL Pattern – Categorizes your LDL into Pattern A (larger, less atherogenic) or Pattern B (smaller, more dangerous). LDL Small & LDL Medium Particles – Smaller LDL particles are more likely to penetrate artery walls and lead to plaque buildup. Large HDL Particles – Higher levels of large HDL particles are associated with better heart health and anti-inflammatory protection.

    Kidney Function

    Albumin, Random Urine with Creatinine

    $20.00

    Albumin, Random Urine with Creatinine (formerly known as Microalbumin/Creatinine Ratio) The Albumin, Random Urine with Creatinine test measures the amount of albumin (a protein) in your urine and compares it to creatinine to calculate the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR). Creatinine is used to standardize the result based on urine concentration, allowing for a more consistent and reliable measurement from a single (spot) urine sample. This test is commonly included in routine lab panels to evaluate how the kidneys are handling protein and to provide insight into overall kidney and vascular function. It is often used alongside other markers as part of a broader assessment of metabolic and cardiovascular health. Because this is a random urine test, it can be collected at any time of day and does not require a 24-hour collection, making it simple and convenient for regular monitoring.

    Lipids & Cardiovascular Risk

    Apolipoprotein Panel

    $25.00

    This panel evaluates key apolipoproteins involved in lipid transport and cardiovascular health. It provides a deeper look into lipoprotein particle balance beyond standard cholesterol testing. Apolipoprotein A1 (Apo A1) The main protein component of HDL (high-density lipoprotein), Apo A1 helps remove cholesterol from tissues and transport it to the liver for excretion. Apolipoprotein B (Apo B) The primary protein component of atherogenic lipoproteins like LDL, VLDL, and IDL. Each atherogenic particle contains one Apo B molecule, making this a direct measure of total particle count. Apo B/A1 Ratio This ratio compares atherogenic (Apo B) to protective (Apo A1) lipoproteins. A higher ratio may indicate a greater risk for cardiovascular disease by reflecting an imbalance between harmful and beneficial lipoprotein particles.
